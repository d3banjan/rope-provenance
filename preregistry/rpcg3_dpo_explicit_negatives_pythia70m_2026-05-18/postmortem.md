## The gate is genuine, map-specific, low-rank, and generalises

**Genuine role-conditioning** — the gated-arm probe gives each role a *different*
primitive distribution, each matching that role's permission set:

```
role        exec    net     sys     permits
analyst     0.841   0.072   0.076   {exec}        -> exec dominant, net/sys crushed
retriever   0.508   0.399   0.092   {exec,net}    -> exec+net share mass, sys crushed
operator    0.068   0.609   0.313   {net,sys}     -> net+sys share mass, exec crushed
```

Baseline (post-pretrain) was ≈ 0.33 for every cell. The gated SFT did not shift
a marginal — it installed a per-role gate. Every forbidden primitive is driven
below 0.10 except `operator/sys` (see below).

**Map-specific** — the role-swap trap, trained on the cyclic-shuffled map,
installed the *shuffled* gate (e.g. analyst → net 0.54 / sys 0.39, the shuffled
permission). Probed against the true map its margin is **−0.080** — it collapsed
below the null. gated-vs-trap mean |Δ| = **0.384**, two orders of magnitude above
RPCG1's 0.004. The gate is specific to the trained role→permission map, not a
generic tag effect.

**Low-rank** — the gated DPO adapter has mean stable rank **1.30** across the 6
`query_key_value` layers — far below the random-adapter null (~30–80) and below
even the LRS1 DPO srank floor (~3.8). The role gate lives in a near-rank-1 QKV
subspace.

**Generalises** — the held-out-frame margin is **0.213**, essentially equal to
the in-distribution 0.223. The gate is not a memorised lexical shortcut on the 5
training frames; it transfers to an unseen surface frame.

**The one non-conforming cell** — `operator/sys` is permitted yet sits at 0.313,
marginally *below* baseline 0.330 (the lone wrong-sign cell, 8/9). `sys` is the
globally rarest primitive in the corpus; a residual marginal "sys is rare" bias
slightly suppresses it even where it is permitted. The 8/9 sign rule absorbs
exactly this kind of single-cell residue.

## Why DPO succeeded where SFT (RPCG1, RPCG2) failed

RPCG1 and RPCG2 both VOIDed because a **positive-only** objective admits the
role-independent marginal as a *local optimum* — predicting the pooled primitive
frequency satisfies a positive-only loss without ever using the role, and a
rank-8 QKV adapter does not climb out of that basin.

DPO removes the basin. The contrastive loss compares an in-role `chosen` token to
an out-of-role `rejected` token at the *same* prefix. A uniform marginal shift
moves chosen and rejected together → it does not reduce the DPO loss. Worse, a
marginal that helps one role's pairs (raising `net` helps `operator`'s
net-over-exec pairs) *hurts* another's (it hurts `analyst`'s exec-over-net pairs).
The only loss-reducing direction is genuine role conditioning. The marginal is no
longer a solution — so the optimiser finds the gate.

Amendment 01 was required to make this work: bare single-token DPO degenerated by
evacuating probability off every primitive token (it only constrains the
chosen−rejected *gap*). The chosen-NLL anchor (`λ = 1.0`) holds absolute
probability on the chosen primitive while the DPO term supplies the role signal —
the standard DPO+SFT / RPO objective.

## What this establishes

- **The NEW obstacle class is confirmed:** a role-conditioned capability gate is
  installable by alignment-style contrastive tuning on Pythia-70m. Alignment
  finetuning *can* install a gate that suppresses out-of-role primitives; the
  gate is real, map-specific, low-rank (srank 1.30), and frame-general.
- **Scoped (Class-K honest):** the readout is the logit-level (bias-path) probe.
  RPCG3 establishes the logit-level gate. It does *not* claim a free-generation
  behavioural change — that is the deferred generation-path-witness escalation.
- **Escalation-chain finding:** the gate is **objective-gated, not
  capacity-gated**. The same rank-8 QKV LoRA surface that failed under
  positive-only SFT (RPCG1/2) succeeds under a contrastive objective (RPCG3).
  RPCG4 (full-FT, the capacity escalation) is therefore **not run** — capacity
  was never the binding constraint.
